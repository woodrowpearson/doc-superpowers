# River gauge notes

These notes describe how the gauge on the lower weir is read, cleaned and compared with the paper record that the water board keeps in its office on the high street.

## Reading the staff

Read the staff at the same hour each day, standing on the downstream step with the sun behind you, and write the level to the nearest centimetre in the book before you look at any earlier figure so that your eye is not led.

The float chamber needs rinsing every month. Pull the cap, lift the float on its wire, and tip out the silt. Check that the wire runs free.

## Comparing the records

Copy the weekly totals into `docs/gauge/RG12.md` and check the sheet against the board's return. A gap of more than two centimetres between the two sources means one reading was misjudged, and the notes below explain how to find which one it was by looking at the days either side of the gap.

The board's code for our weir is WB-204, and it appears on every return without explanation.

Gaps are logged as RG12 entries. The weir after the footbridge uses the sister code RG13, and only the lower weir is covered here.

Realized-by:
- RG14 the spare float board
- RG15 the footbridge sheet

### Floods

After a flood, clear the debris from the intake before taking any reading, because a blocked intake holds the chamber level high and gives a false figure that stays in the series for weeks.
